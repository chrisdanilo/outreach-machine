### 🔴 Subject line — the naming convention, REVISED 2026-08-14

> 🔴 **THE 08-13 RULE IS REPLACED.** **Chris chose the one-branch rule at review on 2026-08-14,
> after a sixth consecutive hand-shortening. The exact wording of his answer is not on record — it
> was given in a multiple-choice prompt that the session transcript does not capture — so this rule
> is sourced to what he DID, not to what he said.**
>
> **The old rule's second branch was the problem.** It said to print the full formal name whenever
> an org publishes no short brand — and Chris then shortened it by hand every single time, most
> recently turning *"at Early Learning Coalition of Osceola County"* into *"at [Org 0567]"*.
> **Six shortenings across six batches is not an exception pattern, it is the rule.**

**The rule now has ONE branch: always shorten, always end with ` . . .`** (space, dot, space, dot,
space, dot).

| Case | What to use | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| The org uses an acronym or short brand about itself | **That short form** + ` . . .` | `at ACCORD . . .` · `at DCCNY . . .` · `at ELCOC . . .` · `at ELCHC . . .` · `at ELCPBC . . .` |
| The org uses **no** short brand about itself | **A plain shortening of its own formal name** + ` . . .` — keep the distinguishing words, drop the boilerplate (`Early Learning Coalition of`, `Child Care Council of`, `, Inc.`) | `at [Org 0567] . . .` · `at [Org 0552] . . .` · `at Child Care Dutchess . . .` |

**Shortening is not the same as inventing.** The old warning still holds for *brands*: never invent
an acronym an organisation does not use about itself. But **trimming the org's own formal name down
to its distinguishing words is now expected**, not forbidden — `Early Learning Coalition of Osceola
County` → `[Org 0567]` is Chris's own shortening, taken from his 2026-08-14 send.

`display_name` is still recorded at the Check-1 read: capture **both** the org's self-used short
brand (if any) **and** its formal name, so the shortening can be derived without a second lookup.

*Historical note:* the two long subjects sent on 2026-08-13 — `at Child Care Coordinating Council of
the North Country` and `at Sullivan County Child Care Council` — are what the old rule produced.
Under this rule they would have been `at [Org 0131] . . .` and `at Sullivan County
CCC . . .`.


### The rules for it

| | |
|---|---|
| **Placement** | Its own line, **directly under the greeting**, with a blank line either side. Before the ask sentence |
| **Wording** | `(I think I got <First Name>'s email address wrong, so I'm not including him/her on this)` |
| 🔴 **Pronoun** | **This is the [Org 0934] the copy carries a pronoun.** [[method]]'s never-assume-a-pronoun rule still binds. If the person's own bio or signature does not state one, **rewrite to avoid it**: `…so I'm not including them on this` |
| **Greeting** | Drops the bounced person. `Jackie and Ryan,` → `Hey [Person 0245],` |
| **When** | Only when a recipient who was on Email 1 **as sent** has hard-bounced. Never for someone merely removed |
| **How many** | One line covers one person. Two bounces → `(I think I got Ryan's and Pat's email addresses wrong, so I'm not including them on this)` |

**Why it exists, in Chris's reasoning:** three people on a thread is partly insurance — *"if we're
wrong and one email bounces, we still have 2 recipients on the thread who might keep each other
accountable to replying."* **The parenthetical protects that mechanic.** Without it, the remaining
recipients see a name silently disappear between emails, which reads as either a mistake being hidden
or a deliberate exclusion. **Naming it plainly costs one line and keeps the thread honest.**
